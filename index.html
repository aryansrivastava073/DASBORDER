DAS Border Sensing Simulator

Building terrain and receiver array…

What this is

A fibre-optic cable is buried along a fenced border. Ground vibration stretches the glass, which the interrogator at the end of the cable detects and locates. This simulator shows what that sensing layer sees, and what can be worked out from it.

SIMULATED DATA Every signal here is synthesised from a physical model. Nothing was recorded in the field, and the accuracy figures quoted elsewhere in this project come from a separate training pipeline, not from this page.

Try this first

  1. Press Start. A heavy vehicle crosses the line.
  2. Press Sound on. The band is pitched up eight times so you can hear it.
  3. Switch the source to Animal and listen to the difference.
  4. Drag Ambient noise upward until the verdict gives up.
  5. Change Receivers from 8 to 96 and watch the position error fall.

Then try guess mode

The class is hidden and you call it from the trace, the spectrum and the sound. A perfect player scores about 89 percent, because roughly a fifth of rounds land in the 10 to 15 Hz band where animal and person genuinely overlap. Those are the cases a trained model is needed for.

Reading the panels

  • Channel response is the amplitude at each receiver. Its shape fixes the position along the line.
  • Trace is the waveform at the strongest channel, drawn as a seismic wiggle with filled polarity.
  • Spectrum is a real 512-point FFT of that trace. The verdict is the spectral peak matched to the nearest band, which is a baseline method.

Getting around

Drag to rotate, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Arrow keys also rotate, + and - zoom, H reopens this panel.

How the reading is made

The trace is synthesised from the source frequency, its harmonics, distance attenuation and noise, sampled at 500 Hz. The spectrum is a real 512-point FFT of that trace, and the verdict comes from its peak matched to the nearest band. A baseline method, not the trained ensemble.
